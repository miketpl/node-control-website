Network topology mapping for L2, L3 and WAN

Node Control is a desktop app for macOS and Windows that draws L2, L3 and WAN topology maps, and can discover neighbours from a seed IP. Professional is £25 a month or £255 a year, with no per-device fees. A 14-day trial, with no card needed, includes L3 and WAN maps and every Engineer task, with no device cap. After 14 days, Free stays for up to 25 devices and still draws the L2 map. In the 14-day trial, Discover shows a clickable example view, but it does not run until Professional.

The problem a map has to solve

A useful map has to match the network you are standing in, not a drawing from last year. You need the switch fabric, the routing overlay, and the links between sites, built from what the devices report: MAC tables, LLDP neighbours, CDP on Cisco, and routing and WAN protocols. You also need a way to walk a site you have not documented yet.

How Node Control does it

The L2 Topology Builder reads each MAC address table, the LLDP neighbours, and CDP on Cisco, and draws an HTML map of switch-to-switch connectivity. It runs on Free, Professional, and in the 14-day trial. Free is limited to 25 devices. Right-click a device on the L2, L3 and WAN maps for SSH, ping and traceroute. On the L2 map, right-click a switch for its MAC table, grouped by VLAN, plus VLANs, spanning tree and trunk ports. Palo Alto and Meraki devices appear as L2 link endpoints, and are mapped properly in the L3 view.

The L3 Topology Builder is in the 14-day trial, then in Professional. It reads routing tables, OSPF and BGP neighbours, and draws which routers and firewalls peer. The default protocols are OSPF and BGP; EIGRP and static routes can be included. Edges are colour-coded, and several routing instances, including Palo Alto logical routers and Cisco VRFs, are unioned and annotated. Watcher of The WAN is the cross-site map of BGP, IPsec and MPLS links, also in the trial, then in Professional.

Discover is a separate page in Professional. It is not an Engineer task. Give it a seed IP and it walks CDP neighbours on Cisco and LLDP neighbours hop by hop, including devices not yet in your library. The map updates live, with an optional ping sweep. You right-click a node to choose what to add. In the 14-day trial, Discover shows a clickable example view, but the walk does not run until Professional.

Each run writes a self-contained HTML file on your machine, with configs, inventory and maps saved locally. The app talks to our server to check its licence and look for updates, plus anonymous usage counts you can switch off. On Professional, Monitor can colour L2 cables for Cisco IOS, IOS-XE and NX-OS ports. Lab Export, in the trial and then in Professional, can turn a saved map into a Cisco CML 2.10 or EVE-NG Community 6.2 lab.

What you get

L2, L3 and WAN maps in one desktop app. L2 stays in the Free Edition for up to 25 devices. L3, WAN and every Engineer task are in the 14-day trial, with no device cap and no card needed. Discover starts from a seed IP and walks neighbours automatically in Professional. In the 14-day trial it shows a clickable example view, but the walk does not run until Professional. Requirements and supported platforms

The app runs on macOS and Windows. There is no Linux build, and no agent on the devices. L2 and L3 maps run against the library; Discover starts from a seed IP. L2 topology works on Cisco IOS, IOS-XE and NX-OS, HP ProCurve, Aruba AOS-CX and OS-Switch, Juniper Junos, Arista EOS, Dell OS6 and OS10, and Extreme EXOS and VSP. On Arista EOS it does not use the MAC table. L3 support includes Cisco, Palo Alto PAN-OS, Juniper and Arista. WAN support also includes Velocloud and Viptela. Pure Layer 2 devices do not appear on the L3 map.

FAQ

Is Layer 2 topology mapping in the Free Edition?

Yes. It runs on Free, on Professional, and in the 14-day trial. After the trial, Free stays for up to 25 devices, with no time limit.

Are L3 and WAN maps in the 14-day trial?

Yes, with every Engineer task and all reports. In the 14-day trial, Discover shows a clickable example view, but the walk does not run until Professional.

How does Node Control find neighbours?

The L2 map reads MAC tables, LLDP neighbours, and CDP on Cisco, for devices already in the library. Discover, in Professional, walks those neighbours hop by hop from a seed IP. In the 14-day trial, Discover shows a clickable example view.

Where are the maps saved?

Each run writes a timestamped HTML file on your machine. Re-running within about ten minutes reuses cached data unless you tick Skip cache.

Does it work on a mixed-vendor network?

Yes. The supported families are listed above. Palo Alto and Meraki devices show as L2 link endpoints and are mapped in the L3 view.
